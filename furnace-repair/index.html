Every time your thermostat calls for heat, the furnace works through a fixed checklist. The inducer motor starts and proves draft by closing a pressure switch, the hot surface igniter heats up, the gas valve opens, the burners light, and the flame sensor confirms there is fire. Only after all of that does the blower begin sending warm air into the house. Finding the step that fails is most of the job. Around Union City, CA, the usual failure points are an oxide-coated flame sensor, an igniter cracked by years of heating and cooling, a pressure switch that stays open because of a weak inducer or a clogged condensate trap on a high-efficiency unit, and control boards with burned relays. Any one of them halts the startup, and from the hallway they all look alike.

Union City HVAC repairs furnaces by testing the sequence, not guessing at it. Our experienced technicians watch the startup step by step and measure at each stage: igniter resistance, flame signal in microamps, pressure switch operation against actual inducer draft, gas valve voltage, and blower and capacitor health. Every visit includes the safety fundamentals on a fuel-burning appliance: heat exchanger inspection, leak-testing of gas connections, and flue draft verification, because the difference between a furnace problem and a furnace hazard is not visible from the thermostat. We measure temperature rise against the data plate before we leave so the fire and the airflow are matched the way the manufacturer intended.

Union City HVAC services natural gas, propane, and electric furnaces of every major brand, from standard 80 percent units to condensing high-efficiency models with their traps, drain lines, and dual pressure switches. Warning Signs for Furnace Repair

Spotting the early warning signs gives you the chance to act before the situation spirals into something far more expensive. These are the signals our technicians see most often when a problem is underway:

  • Flames that start, last a few seconds, and go out again and again
  • A flame that burns soft and yellow rather than steady blue
  • Clicking and a glowing igniter with no burner flame
  • Frequent on-off cycling that never reaches the thermostat setting
  • Loud booms at startup caused by delayed ignition
  • A fan that keeps running while the registers blow cool air
  • Squeals, scrapes, or grinding noises behind the blower door
  • Rust streaks, soot, or water around the cabinet or flue

Why Hire a Professional for Furnace Repair?

The most serious furnace problem is usually hidden. A cracked or corroded heat exchanger can let combustion gases into the airstream, and nothing about it is visible from the outside of the cabinet. Delayed ignition makes things worse, because each boom at light-off puts more stress on that metal. Add high voltage, a gas valve, and a flue to the same box, and the margin for error shrinks. Diagnosis is full of traps too. A high limit that keeps tripping could be a bad switch, or it could be doing exactly its job because a filter is clogged or the blower is dying, and replacing a good switch only hides the real fault.

Our experienced technicians treat every symptom as a question about the whole system. Flame signal gets measured rather than assumed, so a healthy sensor is never replaced while a cracked igniter or weak gas valve skates by. Draft is verified at the pressure switch and the flue. Temperature rise is checked against the data plate, which is how airflow problems get caught before they shorten the exchanger's life. And the gas side is leak-tested with instruments, not a sniff and a shrug. The result is a furnace that has been proven safe and correct under a full cycle, and an honest report if the unit is nearing the point where replacement beats another repair.

How to Prevent Furnace Repair Problems

Smart maintenance is almost always cheaper and easier than an early replacement. Start with these practical measures our team relies on:

  • Keep the condensate trap and drain clear on high-efficiency units to prevent lockouts
  • Avoid packing the utility room, because burners need open space for combustion air
  • Clear boxes, lint, and above all chlorine-based cleaners out of the furnace area
  • Listen at light-off; clean ignition is quiet, and booming means the burners need service
  • Book a fall tune-up so weak igniters and fouled sensors are caught before the first freeze
  • Change the filter monthly during heating season; it is the cheapest furnace part you will ever buy
  • Put a carbon monoxide detector on every level and test them monthly
